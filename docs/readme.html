README Plan Tracker

Plan your paddocks with your farm’s own past, seen from space

Farmers outline their paddocks on a satellite map. Manami the sheep reads what grew there over the last five seasons from NASA satellites, adds soil, slope and 30 years of NASA weather, and plans the next ten seasons of crop rotations.

NASA HLS · Landsat + Sentinel-2 NASA POWER No login or API key React 19 + Vite

What makes it different

NASA Earth data that answers farmers’ real questions

Other rotation planners ask the farmer to type in what grew where, then guess. This one reads the farm’s own record from space and uses it.

1 · Paddock Memory from Space

ProblemCrop records are missing, especially on newly bought or leased land, and a plan is only as good as its history.
DoesReads every clear image of the farm over 5 seasons (about 100 images) and names each season’s crop family with a confidence level and its evidence. One click fills in the history. A time-lapse shows the real images.
HLS v2.0 · 30 m · every 2–3 days

2 · Neighbour canola → blackleg warning

ProblemBlackleg spores blow from last year’s canola stubble, including the neighbour’s, which the farmer can’t see on paper. Growers keep about 500 m away.
DoesFinds canola flowering in every field around the farm and maps it. Within 500 m, the planner steers next season’s canola elsewhere and says why.
HLS yellow-flower index

3 · Safest sowing window

ProblemSow too early and frost hits at flowering. Sow too late and heat hits during grain fill. Rules of thumb ignore the farm’s own climate.
DoesReplays 30 years of daily temperatures for every sowing date and crop, then shows the risk curve and the lowest-risk window.
POWER daily · T2M_MIN, T2M_MAX

4 · “Did my rotation work?”

ProblemFarmers rarely see evidence that their rotation pays off on their own farm.
DoesCompares cereals grown after a break crop with cereals after cereals, using satellite vigour relative to the rest of the farm that season, with rainfall and the water-limited yield benchmark for context.
HLSPOWER rain · PRECTOTCORR

Sharp pictures, best-tested analysis

Every picture a farmer looks at is 10 m Sentinel-2 where possible: the same pass, or for a Landsat date the nearest pass that is cloud-free over the farm. The analysis uses whatever tested best. NASA HLS at 30 m found far more canola than Sentinel-2 at 10 m, so it stays. Each paddock is read only from pixels lying wholly inside it, which lifted crop-type accuracy on real fields by 5–17 points.

Everything runs in the browser and images are cached, so a farm is read once (about 30 seconds the first time). Click any screenshot to enlarge it.

The workspace

Built like a tool farmers can run a farm from

Same colours, Lato and Manami as the Figma file, laid out as a proper app: one sidebar, a page per job, and live data instead of loading screens.

Find anything

Ctrl K (or /) searches pages, paddocks and actions: draw a paddock, run the soil check, export the plan, switch theme.

Live, not blocking

The satellite read, soil, slope and climate load in the background. The top bar shows satellite progress, and the plan refines itself as data arrives.

Light and dark

Warm cream by day, soil brown by night, or follow the system. The sidebar collapses to icons for more room.

Manami, on the side

She opens as a side panel with suggested questions for the page, and gives new farmers one short tip per page that never covers anything.

Safe to try things

Every change shows a short notice; deleting a paddock can be undone. Export the farm to a file and import it on another computer.

Guided setup

Six steps with a progress bar and a “Getting started” card in the sidebar. The demo farm skips straight to the Overview.

How accurate is it?

Checked against independent ground truth

Crop from space vs. USDA crop maps

The USDA Cropland Data Layer maps the crop in every 30 m pixel of the US. Region: canola country in Cavalier County, North Dakota. About 5,500 uniform 150 m blocks per season were classified by the app’s own code. 2021 was used while developing. 2020 was held out and only checked at the end.

Where it goes wrong (the app says so too)

  • Cereal vs. pulse is the weakest call. Peas were read as cereal or canola about half the time, and 26% of 2021 wheat as pulse. These are marked “best guess” and never filled in automatically.
  • Sunflower also flowers yellow and was read as canola in 2021. It’s rare in Australian winter cropping.
  • Fallow is sometimes read as a weedy pulse.
  • Thresholds were tuned on 2021 only. A tuned version that fitted 2021 better did worse on 2020, so the simpler rules were kept.
  • Validated in North Dakota, a different climate from Australia. Seasons are set by latitude (southern: April–January).

Tested to pick the best analysis

  • Pure pixels adopted. Reading paddocks only from pixels wholly inside them: crop type right on 20–60 ha fields 62 → 79 % (2021) and 73 → 78 % (2020); over 60 ha 68 → 85 % and 88 → 93 %. npm run validate:pixels
  • 10 m canola map not adopted. Sentinel-2 at 10 m found 46–59 % of canola vs 73–92 % for NASA HLS at 30 m, same detector, field interiors. npm run validate:canola10m

Other checks (Oct 2026)

  • Paddock area within 0.001% of the WGS84 geodesic, 1 ha to 40,000 ha, even at 60° latitude.
  • Soil sample point always inside the paddock, including U-shapes, crescents and split halves.
  • Texture class 22/22 USDA reference cases. SoilGrids units verified against the live API.
  • Slope fit recovers known slopes to ±0.02% and the right facing direction.
  • Drawing: finishes with exactly the clicked corners, rejects crossed outlines and mis-clicks.
  • Sliders move the plan the right way across their whole range (monotone).

Limits worth knowing: SoilGrids is a 250 m model, NASA POWER climate is regional (~50 km), and the elevation model is 90 m. Sowing-window thermal times are typical mid-maturity values for south-eastern Australia, and varieties differ by a few weeks. Frost counts from ≤ 1 °C because a POWER grid cell is milder than a cold paddock floor. The rotation report is a first hint until it has at least two cases on each side.

Data sources

Where every number comes from

NASA

HLS v2.0

Crop history, neighbour canola, vigour. Landsat 8/9 + Sentinel-2 via Microsoft Planetary Computer, which serves NASA’s HLS to browsers without a login. Cached in Cache Storage.

NASA

POWER daily

Sowing window and season rainfall: T2M_MIN, T2M_MAX and PRECTOTCORR for the last 30 complete years, one request per farm, cached.

NASA

POWER climatology

Long-term rainfall, temperature and root-zone soil wetness (PRECTOTCORR, T2M, GWETROOT) on the Soil card.

ISRIC

SoilGrids 2.0

Texture, pH, clay/sand, organic carbon. 250 m, 0–30 cm. Requests are spaced (fair use ≈ 5/min), cached for 30 days, with “Try again” when down.

Open-Meteo

Elevation

Slope, aspect and elevation from the Copernicus 90 m DEM. A plane is fitted across the whole paddock.

Maps

Esri + OpenStreetMap

Esri World Imagery / Topo basemaps, Nominatim place search. Prices in src/data/crops.js are example values only.

Design → code

The Figma palette, grown into a design system

The palette and Lato come from the Figma file. src/styles/tokens.css turns them into semantic tokens (background, surfaces, text, borders, status colours) for a light theme on warm cream and a dark theme on soil brown. Button fills and label colours are kept exactly as designed. The UI kit (src/components/ui.jsx) adds cards, KPI tiles, tables, tabs, toasts, a command palette and SVG charts in the same style; icons are Lucide line icons.

Known gaps

What’s next

Crop from space

Telling cereals from pulses needs more than greenness. Sentinel-1 radar or labelled Australian paddocks (e.g. ABARES land use) would sharpen it.

Sowing window

Let the farmer pick variety maturity (early / mid / late) instead of assuming mid.

Auth & name

No backend yet; sign-in only names the local profile. The app is still called “Webapp Name” (src/config.js, index.html).

Prices

Static examples. Connect a market feed in src/services/.

Soil split

A two-sample heuristic per paddock. Soil-zone mapping from a raster or EM survey would sharpen it.

Live status

Open questions and next steps are kept in the tracker.
